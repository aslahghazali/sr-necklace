Interpretation: 

The figures suggest that the story’s focus changes as the events unfold. At first, the strongest ideas are Mathilde’s dissatisfaction with her ordinary life and her wish to belong to a wealthier social class. She dreams of elegant rooms, fine food, beautiful clothes, and jewellery. These details show that she connects wealth with happiness and respect. Although she is described as beautiful and graceful, she feels that her appearance and social position do not match the life she believes she deserves.

The story then shows how important appearance becomes to Mathilde. She wants to look impressive at the ball and borrow a necklace to complete her appearance. For one evening, she receives the attention and admiration she has hoped for. The necklace seems to help her enter the world she dreams about, but this happiness is temporary. When the necklace disappears, the couple’s attention turns from appearance and pleasure to searching, money, and the cost of replacing it.

The figures also help trace the consequences of the loss. Mathilde and her husband borrow money to buy a replacement necklace, and they spend ten years paying off the debt. Their lives become difficult, and Mathilde changes through years of hard work. The necklace therefore represents more than jewellery: it becomes connected to the couple’s financial burden and the sacrifices they make. The story shows how a single event can change their daily lives for a long time.

The final revelation gives the story its strongest irony. Mathilde learns that the original necklace was imitation jewellery and was worth far less than the replacement they bought. The couple’s hardship came from believing that the necklace was valuable without checking its real worth. This ending changes how we understand the earlier events: the necklace looked expensive, and Mathilde treated it as precious, but its appearance did not match its actual value.

Overall, the figures suggest that the story connects social ambition, appearance, money, and mistaken belief. They show where words related to these ideas appear, while the story itself helps explain why those patterns matter. Mathilde’s desire to be admired leads her to value appearance highly, and the lost necklace turns that desire into years of hardship. The ending reminds us that appearances can be misleading and that our assumptions can have serious consequences.
