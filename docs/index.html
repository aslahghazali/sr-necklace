01 / Abstract

What the analysis asks

This project uses an LLM-assisted phrase analysis to examine how “The Necklace” connects Mathilde’s social ambitions with appearance, money, and mistaken beliefs about value. The story is divided into 13 researcher-defined segments and four narrative stages. Coded phrases are matched to those segments, and counts are shown both as raw hits and hits per 100 words.

The accompanying interpretation reads the patterns alongside the events of the story: Mathilde’s dissatisfaction and fantasies, the ball, the loss and repayment, and the final revelation that the original necklace was imitation jewellery.

02 / Research question

Perceived value and real cost

Research question
How does the story use appearance and money to expose the gap between perceived social value and actual material value?

Claim examined
Mathilde’s longing for wealth makes appearance seem central to her identity; the lost necklace leads to years of hardship, while the ending reveals that this sacrifice rested on a mistaken belief about the jewel’s worth.

03 / Method

From phrases to patterns

The researcher defined segments and stages based on scene shifts, then used a prompt to extract phrases associated with four interpretive dimensions. The project method note says the phrases were generated with Claude, checked against the story, and matched case-insensitively as whole words; longer phrases were matched first and overlapping text counted once. The resulting frequency patterns are descriptive and support interpretation rather than prove literary meaning.

01 · Aspiration

Class, rank, luxury, envy, and longing for a wealthier life.

02 · Appearance

Beauty, clothing, adornment, and the identity Mathilde presents.

03 · Loss & debt

The missing necklace, replacement, borrowing, and repayment.

04 · Irony

False assumptions about the necklace’s material value.

04 / Interpretation

How the story’s focus shifts

The figures place the strongest class-aspiration and appearance signals near the opening, where Mathilde imagines the life she believes she deserves. After the necklace disappears, the narrative turns toward searching, cost, and debt. At the close, the revelation reframes the earlier sacrifice: the replacement was expensive, but the original was imitation.

Across the story, the necklace becomes more than an accessory. It links Mathilde’s wish to be admired with the couple’s years of financial hardship, while the ending exposes how appearance and assumption can distort judgments of value.

06 / Limitations

Reading the counts carefully

Phrase counts are selective

Counts reflect the chosen phrase categories and matching rules. They do not capture every expression of a theme or its full context.

Segments vary in length

Normalising hits per 100 words helps comparison, but short segments can still make rates sensitive to a few matches.

Interpretation needs the story

Repeated words alone cannot establish character motivation, irony, or literary significance. The narrative context remains essential.

Descriptive, not inferential

The report notes that phrase hits within passages are not independent; the statistical summaries are descriptive rather than formal evidence of causation.
