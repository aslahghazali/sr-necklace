# Initial impression: "The Necklace" (Guy de Maupassant, 1884)

## Sampling methodology
I split the story into three parts of about 74 sentences each. From each part I read one block of 15 sentences: the first block of Part 1, a randomly placed block of Part 2 (seed 42), and the last block of Part 3. The blocks cover Mathilde's dissatisfaction with her life, the search after the necklace disappears, and the final revelation.

## My impression
The opening block presents Mathilde as someone who feels that her beauty and taste belong in a wealthier world. The descriptions of elegant rooms, fine dinners and jewels make her ordinary home feel like a source of suffering. In the second block, the missing necklace shifts the story from longing to panic: Mathilde and her husband search their clothes and retrace their route, but cannot find it. In the final block, Mathilde explains that she replaced the necklace and spent ten years paying for it. Madame Forestier's reply—that the original was an imitation worth at most five hundred francs—makes the sacrifice seem painfully disproportionate. I came away thinking about how a belief in an object's value can shape a person's life.

## One overall concept
The idea that joins my marked phrases is **perceived value**: the characters' beliefs about wealth, appearance and the necklace guide their choices, even when those beliefs do not match reality.

## My research problem
In these sampled blocks, descriptions of luxury establish Mathilde's dissatisfaction; the missing necklace creates a financial crisis; and the ending reveals a sharp difference between the necklace's perceived and actual value. My question is: How does the story use appearance and money to expose the gap between perceived social value and actual material value? The claim I will test is: Mathilde's longing for wealth makes appearance seem essential to her identity; the lost necklace then turns that longing into years of hardship, while the final revelation shows that the sacrifice was built on a mistaken belief about the jewel's value.

## My dimensions
1. **Class and social aspiration.** The gap between Mathilde's social position and the wealthy life she longs for.
   (a) *status_and_rank*: Words about class, birth, wealth, distinction and social position.
   (b) *luxury_and_display*: Descriptions of fine rooms, clothing, jewels and elegant entertainment.
   (c) *envy_and_longing*: Expressions of desire, regret, fantasy and dissatisfaction.
2. **Appearance and identity.** How clothing, beauty and jewelry shape Mathilde's sense of self and how others see her.
   (a) *beauty_and_charm*: Descriptions of beauty, grace, elegance and attractiveness.
   (b) *clothing_and_adornment*: Dresses, jewels and other things worn for appearance.
   (c) *recognition_and_self_image*: Moments when appearance affects confidence, identity or social recognition.
3. **Debt and material loss.** The financial consequences of losing the necklace and replacing it.
   (a) *money_and_cost*: References to francs, prices, expense and the cost of replacement.
   (b) *search_and_loss*: Language describing the missing necklace and attempts to find it.
   (c) *labor_and_repayment*: Hardship, sacrifice and the years spent paying for the replacement.
4. **Irony and false value.** The contrast between what the necklace seems to be worth and what it is actually worth.
   (a) *assumption_and_certainty*: Statements that show what the characters believe about the necklace.
   (b) *imitation_and_similarity*: Words that blur the distinction between the original and its replacement.
   (c) *reversal_and_revelation*: The ending's disclosure that changes the meaning of the sacrifice.
